Privacy

Draft. Last updated 1 November 2026.

The short version

RamWarden runs on your computer. On the free local tier it sends nothing to us. With a hosted plan, it sends short descriptions of the apps and tabs it is deciding about, our server asks Jev, and the description is forgotten once the answer is back.

What stays on your computer

  • Your process list, memory figures, window focus and activity history.
  • Your browser tabs. The extension talks only to RamWarden on your own machine (localhost), never to our server.
  • Your settings and watchlist.

What a hosted plan sends

Only when memory is short and RamWarden is choosing what to close, and only if you have signed in: for each candidate, its name (an app name, or a tab's title and site), how long it has been idle, how much memory it uses, and one of a fixed set of questions, such as whether you are likely to come back to it within a day. Our server will only forward those fixed questions.

We forward this to TypeSafe, which runs Jev, to get the answer. We do not store request contents and do not write them to logs. Our logs hold a short fingerprint of your device's token, which questions were asked, sizes and timings.

Your account

  • Wallet addresses you sign in with. Signing in costs no gas and gives us no access to your funds.
  • Devices you have linked: a name, the platform and when each was last seen. You can revoke any of them from your account page.
  • Payments: the plan, amount, method and the transaction or Stripe reference, kept for accounting. Card details go to Stripe and never reach us.
  • Usage: how many hosted decisions you used each day, as counts only, kept for about 13 months.

On-chain payments are public by nature: anyone can see a payment from your wallet to RamWarden's checkout.

This website

No analytics, no trackers, no third-party scripts and no cookies. Signing in on this site keeps a session token in your browser tab until you close it.

Contact and deletion

To have your account deleted, or for any privacy question, open an issue on GitHub.
