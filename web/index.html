Meet the wardens.

Each warden is an agent with one job. They share what they see, explain what they do, and never touch what is protected. RAM Warden comes with every plan; new wardens arrive as Pro add-ons.

Cheapest fix first. Closing things last.

Most memory pressure never needs anything closed. RamWarden climbs a ladder ordered by what a wrong call would cost you, and steps back down, undoing what it can, as soon as pressure passes.

  1. 1 · reclaimThe OS compresses cold memoryCosts you a few page faults
  2. 2 · page-outIdle apps' unused pages move outCosts you a few page faults
  3. 3 · tabsIdle browser tabs unloadThe tab reloads when you click it
  4. 4 · suspendApps on your watchlist pauseResumed the moment you're back
  5. 5 · closeWatchlisted apps close, coldest firstA countdown you can cancel
MEASURES

The real number

RamWarden counts memory the way the OS charges it, not summed RSS, which double-counts what a browser shares between its processes. On one machine that difference was 15 GB against 6.5 GB.

TRIGGERS

On stalls, not "percent used"

A full but healthy machine is fine. RamWarden acts on the time your computer actually loses waiting for memory, so it stays out of the way until it matters.

PROTECTS

Some things are never touched

Your desktop, terminals, VMs, security software, games and anti-cheat are off limits whatever any setting says. Pausing and closing only ever apply to apps you put on the watchlist.

Jev decides what you won't miss.

Knowing what's idle is easy. Knowing what you'll come back to isn't. RamWarden asks Jev, TypeSafe's "system one" model, a few narrow questions about each candidate and gets a calibrated answer in a fraction of a second.

ASKS

Will you be back within 24 hours?

Asked of every tab and app RamWarden is thinking of closing. A recipe from last week goes; the docs page you keep returning to stays.

ASKS

Could this hold unsaved work?

A half-filled form, a draft, an editor with changes. If there's a real chance, it isn't closed.

ASKS

Does it need to keep running?

Servers, builds, syncs and terminal sessions look idle but aren't. In testing, the hosted tier spared a localhost dev server that a small local model would have closed.

FALLS BACK

Works without it, too

Offline, signed out or over your daily allowance, RamWarden uses its local estimate. Nothing stops working; decisions are just less sharp.

Questions

What gets sent to your server?

Only with a hosted plan, and only when RamWarden is deciding what to close: a short description of the candidate (app or site name, how long it's been idle, its memory) and the fixed question being asked. The server passes it to Jev and forgets it. Request contents are never stored or logged. On the free local tier nothing is sent anywhere. See the privacy policy.

Will it close something I'm using?

It's built not to. It acts only when memory is actually short, tries compression first, spares anything you used recently, asks whether you'll be back, and only ever pauses or closes apps you've put on the watchlist. Closing starts a countdown you can cancel, and paused apps resume as soon as pressure passes.

Is it safe for games and anti-cheat?

Yes. Games, anti-cheat (Vanguard, EasyAntiCheat), security software and system processes are structurally protected. RamWarden never touches them, and it never weakens security settings to free memory.

What is a warden?

An agent on your computer with one job. RAM Warden handles memory. Wazuh Warden, coming as a Pro add-on, watches for threats using the open-source Wazuh agent. Each warden explains what it found and asks before applying a fix from chat.

Why sign in with a wallet?

It's an account without a password or an email. Signing the sign-in message costs no gas and moves no funds. Your RamWarden account is separate from the wallet, so you can pay from a different wallet or by card.

Do I need the browser extension?

Only for tab unloading. Without it RamWarden still manages apps; with it, tabs and apps are weighed together.

Is it open source?

Yes, under the MIT license. The source is on GitHub, so you can read it or build any version yourself.
